## Background and problem

Mobile banking user behaviour is spread across multiple business stages, with inconsistent definitions and missing fields, making it hard to support business judgement directly. A stable data processing pipeline had to be established before any statistical analysis.

## Constraints

- The data is sensitive and was processed only within a controlled environment, with no raw records shared externally.
- Business fields change with each release, so the cleaning logic had to be maintainable.
- Analysis conclusions had to be understandable to non-technical colleagues.

## Approach and architecture

The workflow has four stages: collection, cleaning, metric calculation and statistical analysis. Field naming and time conventions were unified, reusable cleaning scripts were established, and metrics and reports were then organised around business questions.

## Key decisions

- **Documented definitions**: every metric states its definition and data source, avoiding the same name meaning different things.
- **Layered scripts**: collection, cleaning and analysis are kept separate so each can be rerun independently.
- **Explainable results**: prefer statistical methods the business can understand, with complex models used only as a supplement.

## Results and measurement

Established a reusable workflow for data collection, cleaning and statistical analysis that supports business decisions; specific business metrics are not disclosed on this site.

## Technical details

- Extracted features such as transactions, logins and feature usage from behaviour logs.
- Used Pandas/SQL for cleaning, aggregation and segmented statistics.
- Used classification/clustering results to explain user segments and business differences.

## Reflection

Definition drift across periods is the biggest cost. If I did it again, I would establish a field dictionary and version records before starting the analysis.
