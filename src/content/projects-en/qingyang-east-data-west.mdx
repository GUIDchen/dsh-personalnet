## Background and problem

The "East Data, West Computing" project involves multiple categories of investment — data centres, energy, network and operations — so scattered cost data had to be organised into comparable definitions before the input-output relationship could be assessed.

## Constraints

- Public data is limited, so some costs had to be estimated from industry parameters.
- The project timeline was short (about 3 months), requiring usable conclusions within a limited time.
- The report targets non-technical readers, so conclusions must be traceable.

## Approach and architecture

First establish a cost classification framework (construction, energy, network, operations), then collect and verify data item by item, build a cost estimation model, and finally carry out input-output analysis and scenario comparison.

## Key decisions

- **Classify first**: unify cost categories to avoid double counting.
- **Transparent parameters**: estimation parameters and their sources are listed in the report for review.
- **Scenario analysis**: present baseline, optimistic and conservative scenarios rather than a single-point conclusion.

## Results and measurement

Completed the collection and organisation of cost data, contributed to building the cost estimation model, and independently completed the input-output analysis report, providing a structured basis for decision discussions.

## Technical details

- Broke down cost categories such as data centre, power, network and operations.
- Used a cost estimation model to connect capacity, unit price and scenario assumptions.
- Independently completed the input-output analysis and the report presentation.

## Reflection

The credibility of estimation research depends on the sources of its parameters. If I did it again, I would record a source and confidence level for every parameter and add sensitivity analysis.
