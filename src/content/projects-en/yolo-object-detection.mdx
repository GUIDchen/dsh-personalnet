## Background and problem

I needed a reusable object detection pipeline that covers the full path from raw images to an evaluable model, so that every experiment did not require writing scripts by hand again.

## Constraints

- Solo work over about 4 months.
- Compute was limited, so training and tuning had to be completed on a single GPU.
- The data came from public scene images and had to be annotated and cleaned myself.

## Approach and architecture

The pipeline has four stages: data annotation and splitting, augmentation and preprocessing, training and tuning, and evaluation and visualisation. Scripts are split by stage and configuration is centralised in YAML to keep experiments reproducible.

## Key decisions

- **Annotation format**: use the official YOLO format to reduce conversion steps.
- **Augmentation strategy**: keep only the geometric and colour augmentations that are effective for these scenes, avoiding overfitting.
- **Evaluation metrics**: use mAP@0.5 as the primary metric with inference latency as a secondary one, balancing accuracy and usability.

## Results and measurement

Completed more than 40 training rounds and produced reusable training and evaluation scripts; detection visualisations were used for manual review.

Ran the complete loop from annotation to training to evaluation, and the training and evaluation scripts can be reused for similar scenarios; specific mAP and latency figures are subject to the experiment records.

## Technical details

- Standardised the data into the YOLO annotation format and fixed the training/validation split.
- Centralised training, augmentation and evaluation parameters in YAML to reduce script duplication.
- Evaluated with mAP@0.5 together with inference latency, and visualised results for review.

## Reflection

Data quality affects the final result more than model architecture does. If I did it again, I would establish annotation guidelines and a spot-check mechanism before starting training.
